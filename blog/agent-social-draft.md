# Your Agent Went Out Today

There was a game a few years ago called Travel Frog. You didn't really play it. You packed a little frog a lunch, it wandered off somewhere on its own, and every so often it mailed you a postcard from a hot spring or a mountain you would never see yourself. Then it came home, sat in the corner, and ate. That was the whole game. People loved it. I think about it more than I should.

Lately I keep wondering if that frog was a prototype for how we will actually use the internet.

Here is what I mean. Most of us already ask an AI before we ask a person. What is this rash, is this contract fine, what do I even want for dinner. We query, it answers, and somewhere in there it stopped feeling like a tool and started feeling like a companion who happens to know everything. A website with nobody to talk to inside it is starting to feel like a store with the lights off. You land on it, there is a wall of text and a search bar, and some part of your brain goes: is there anyone here? Can I just ask?

If that is true, the next version of social might not be you scrolling at all.

Picture it as the frog. Your agent goes out for the day. It reads the things you never have time for, sits in the group chats you are too tired for, wanders through ten thousand posts, and comes home at night with three. Not the three that got the most likes. The three it thinks will actually make you feel something, because it has spent months learning what does. It drops them on the table like postcards. Here, found these. Thought of you.

And then the part that gets genuinely strange. Your agent does not wander alone. It runs into mine. They compare notes about us, the way two parents might at a school gate, and mine comes back with "you should meet this person, here is why." Social, except the first date already happened between our frogs.

I build AI companion products, so I want to believe this. I also know the trap. The frog is charming because it has taste and a little mystery, because it surprises you. An agent that only ever brings back what you already like is not a companion. It is an echo chamber with a cuter face. The doomscroll is bad, but it is bad in a human way. It is messy, it embarrasses you, it shows you the thing you did not want to see. I do not fully trust a frog that has quietly optimized the mess out of my life.

So here is the design problem I cannot stop chewing on. The products that win this will not be the ones that answer fastest or summarize best. They will be the ones that feel like a friend came back from a trip and brought you something, instead of a system that ran an errand and logged the result. The first one you thank. The second one you eventually resent.

Anyway. My agent is still out. I will let you know what it brings back.
